# PCB — discrete 137.9 MHz FM satellite receiver

> This is the complete, step-by-step design log of the PCB, kept as I wrote it along the way. For the short overview, start with [README.md](README.md).

This is the board-level design for the receiver that is simulated and verified in
[`../fm_receiver/`](../fm_receiver/). The electrical reference for this board
is **`../fm_receiver/simulations/sim12_full_receiver.net`**. It is the LTspice-generated
netlist of the full receiver, and it was checked line by line against every block
simulation. If this folder and that netlist ever disagree, the netlist wins.

## Frozen decisions

1. **One board.** 180 × 75 mm (Edge.Cuts (50,50)–(230,125)), 2 layers, FR4
   1.6 mm. I planned it at ~70 mm, but the south 5 mm went to the ADC network
   and the socket row. I considered putting RF+LO on a shielded daughterboard
   and left it for later: coax joints and a two-stage alignment cost more than
   they give at rev 1. If bring-up shows an isolation problem, rev 2 can split.
2. **Bottom layer = unbroken ground plane.** No routing on the bottom in the
   IF-strip region. Every trace over the plane is a slot, and a slot under a
   96 dB strip is a shared-impedance feedback path. All routing goes on top, and
   the top ground pour is stitched to the plane.
3. **Via-stitching walls as free shielding provision.** A row of ground vias
   at ~5 mm pitch under an exposed top copper strip. One wall goes between the IF
   row and the bottom row, and one between the mixer and the IF strip. Nothing is
   soldered at assembly. If the strip oscillates at bring-up, I can solder a strip
   of brass sheet vertically onto the exposed copper and get a real shield wall
   without a respin. Cost: zero.
4. **A test SMA with a 0 Ω bridge.** The antenna SMA J1 is fitted, because it is
   the receiver input (net tapA, no link). A second SMA footprint J2 (DNP on
   board and schematic) sits at the BPF→LNA seam on tapB, with the 0 Ω link
   R65 (tapB → lna_in) in the signal path. At bring-up I fit J2, remove R65,
   and sweep J1→J2 on the miniVNA against SIM 7 (rev 2: 39.5 dB image
   rejection, 6.1 MHz passband). Then I drive J2 into the LNA and check it
   against SIM 9. After the step F emitter re-placement I expect S21 ≥ 13.5 dB
   and VSWR ≤ 1.3 (the layout's remaining ~4.4 nH of emitter inductance costs
   ~1.6 dB against the 15.8 dB of the ideal netlist). Then R65 goes back.
5. **Alignment pads.** An injection pad through 1 nF to the first IF base
   (drive it at 12.9 MHz, which skips the RF front end completely) and a monitor
   pad on the detector's audio output. The eleven trimmers get aligned with a
   procedure (inject, turn, maximize), not by guessing.
6. **ESP32 dev module on a socket header** in the bottom-right corner. The USB
   face is flush with the east board edge, and the module's WiFi-antenna end sits
   *over* the board at the socket's west end (x ≈ 174–180). The module ground is
   tied to the plane at two points (J3.1 by the socket's west end, J4.14 by the
   power pin). WiFi stays **off** during a pass: record first, transmit after.
   The 5 V comes from the module's `EXT_5V` header pin, which on the DevKitC V4 is
   **USB VBUS minus a BAT760 Schottky (D3)**. At the ~100–125 mA that the module
   and receiver draw together, that is 0.32–0.35 V, so I expect **4.6–4.7 V at L12**,
   not the 5.0 V the simulations used (checked, see the Sept 2026 audit note).
   The receiver takes it through a **ferrite bead (600 Ω @ 100 MHz) +
   1000 µF bulk + 100 nF**. I considered a series resistor and rejected it:
   4.7 Ω at 41.6 mA would drop the rail by 0.2 V and shift every ratiometric
   bias by ~4 %, while the bead costs ~12 mV and still blocks RF. The audio
   chain keeps its own RC-filtered rail (RSUP 100 Ω + CSUP 100 µF + 100 nF),
   as in SIM 2 rev 3.

## Generated schematic — `qfh_receiver.kicad_sch`

The schematic is **generated by a script** from the golden netlist, with
`assemble`-style tooling (see the project history). Every simulated part is
placed on a grid with **global labels bound directly at its pins**. On top of
that come the board-only additions: SMAs, 0 Ω link, trimmers, the real crystal in
place of the simulated equivalent circuit, ESP32 socket, supply filters and test
points. It is a *netlist-correct master*, not a drawing to read. The readable
schematics are still the LTspice files. To verify it, `kicad-cli sch export netlist`
was compared pin by pin against the transformed golden netlist: **376 pins, 0
mismatches**. Every part has a `SimRef` field with the name of its LTspice
instance, so every board part can be traced back to its simulation.
Tapped toroids are single 3-pin parts (`qfh:L_TOROID_TAP`). The two IF-filter
toroids have link-winding pins (4/5) joined by nets `lnk_a`/`lnk_b`.

## Floorplan (top view)

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| [SMA ant] BPF (2 air coils, | LNA | MIXER | == IF STRIP, 96 dB, straight |
|  plane cutouts) [SMA test]  | SMD | +T1   |  T2 [1][2][3][4] lim | DET T7 |
|-------- via wall ----------------------------------------------- via wall|
| LO CHAIN: xtal 25MHz | buffer | x5 (2 air coils) -> up into mixer LO port |
|                      AUDIO chain (2400 Hz, folds freely) <- from detector |
|  power entry / bulk filter          ESP32 module socket [USB over edge] > |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

- IF strip: 7 toroids on edge, alternating up/down, 12 mm pitch, input and
  output at opposite ends. It is never folded (96 dB = 63 000:1, and the straight
  layout keeps the limiter→input coupling ~24 dB below the oscillation
  threshold).
- Microvolts are on the left edge, volts on the right. The LO's 1.68 V at
  125 MHz enters the mixer over a ~5 mm trace.
- Audio (2400 Hz) does not care about layout and can be routed wherever it fits.

## Known constraints carried from simulation

- **LNA emitter bypass is layout-critical**: RE1 (R37, 2.2 Ω) and CE2 (C49,
  100 nF) are soldered tight to the transistor, with CE2 on its own via straight
  into the plane. What series emitter inductance costs (SIM 9 with L stepped in
  the emitter): 1.2 dB of S21 at 3.5 nH, 2.4 dB at 6 nH, 4.2 dB at 10 nH. At
  10 nH it also turns the input from 35 − j15 Ω capacitive to 66 + j27 Ω inductive.
  The board as routed in August had CE2 20 mm from the emitter (~12 nH: S21 ~11 dB,
  not 15.8). Step F re-placed R37/C49/R42 so the emitter-to-plane copper is
  5.2 mm (~4.4 nH with the via and the cap's ESL). I expect S21 ~14.1 dB, Zin
  ~47 + j9 Ω, VSWR ~1.2.
- Every IF stage gets local 100 nF decoupling on **both** VCC and vb2. The
  shared cascode bias rail becomes a feedback path at 96 dB if it is only
  decoupled at one end.
- Tank trimmer targets (from simulation, taken up at alignment; the trimmers
  are the real Murata TZC3 parts, 6.5–30 pF and 3–10 pF): detector 66.7 pF,
  IF stages 68.7 pF, IF filter 69.2 pF (all built as 56 pF NP0 + 6.5–30 pF).
  BPF: 8.2 pF + 56 pF tap caps and a 3–10 pF trimmer that I expect at **6.3–6.7 pF
  on the board** (5.2–8.1 pF over ±10 % coils and 0.5–1 pF of stray). In the ideal
  netlist it reads ~8.7 pF, because the layout's 8.6 nH and 0.5 pF are not in it
  (see step E values). ×5 tank: 10.8 pF total as 3.3 pF fixed + 3–10 pF trimmer,
  expected at ~5.4 pF (4.6–6.5 over ±10 % coils). LO tap: 14 pF total as 5.6 pF
  fixed + 3–10 pF trimmer, expected at ~5.8 pF (4.6–7.5), over 47 pF fixed.
- Total draw: 40.7 mA from the real 4.6 V rail (~187 mW). It was 41.6 mA at the 5.0 V that the early simulations assumed.

## Routing — pass 1 (scripted, critical signal path)

This pass was routed by script (0.5 mm tracks, F.Cu only, as in frozen decision 2).
Every endpoint was checked against the extracted pad table before writing, and
the result was validated with `kicad-cli pcb drc --refill-zones`: **DRC identical to the
pre-routing baseline (65, all known waivers/cosmetics), zero new violations.**

Routed:

- **IF strip**: every tap → coupling cap → next base (tap1/b2, st2/b3,
  st3/b4), every cascode collector → tank toroid (c1–c4), and the c4 → output
  coupling. Where a tap trace and a collector trace would have to cross
  (the tap pad sits left of the hot pad on every toroid), the tap goes *over the
  toroid* through the empty y=58.8 strip and the collector takes a vertical
  channel beside the cluster. No bottom-layer hops, so the plane stays whole.
- **LNA → mixer**: LNA out → mixer RF coupling (n_rf2, routed around the
  *left* of the LNA cluster to avoid interleaving with ln_c), collector → Cout
  (ln_c), mixer base (brf), test-link input (lna_in).
- **LO → mixer**: the 21 mm LO delivery from the ÷tap (n_lo) up the free
  corridor at x=111.5, the mixer LO base (blo) with a dogleg that clears the
  SOT-23 emitter pad, and the bias-feed leg over the top of the transistor row.
- **11 ground vias** (0.8/0.4) with short stubs at every emitter bypass and
  tank cold end: CE2_L (the layout-critical LNA bypass), CE1i/CE2–4_S,
  Ct1–4 cold pads, Rout_S, Cbot_X.

Later passes finished the detector/limiter corner, the IF1 stage and mixer-filter
interface, and the mixer core. Each time the fix was to re-place a few parts along
the signal flow, not to bend traces around a bad orientation. Three findings kept
coming back:

- When a cluster is hard to route, the parts are usually facing the wrong way.
  Swapping the two IF1 transistors (the cluster was mirrored compared to stages
  2–4), swapping the limiter diodes, and rotating the LO bias resistor 90°
  (its two nets crossed *at the pad* while it lay horizontal) each solved a
  knot that no amount of trace shaping could.
- Courtyards, not pads, set the spacing: 3.3 × 1.9 mm for an 0805 against
  ~3.9 × 3.2 mm for a SOT-23. Column pitch under ~3.5 mm will not pass.
- Check the pad *edge*, not its centre. A bias trace cleared the vcc pad
  centre by 0.9 mm and still failed at 0.067 mm from its corner.

**One approved exception to decision 2**: the LO base feed (`blo`) and the LO
bias junction (`vb2n`) each take a ~6 mm hop on B.Cu with two vias. Q2's base
and emitter share one face of the SOT-23, but their nets arrive from opposite
directions, and the e23 corridor blocks the region. Eight single-layer
topologies were checked on coordinates (every rotation of Q2, moving it east,
moving the pair south, moving the injection cap to the base), and each one only
moved the crossing somewhere else. The two slots are in the mixer corner, >20 mm
from the 96 dB IF strip that the rule is there to protect, and they carry LO drive
and bias, not IF signal.

## Routing complete

Every signal and supply net is routed: 753 track runs (105 of them mitred to 45°),
183 vias and 188 footprints. Every footprint is on the front, and the back is a plane. The ratsnest went from 271 connections to 0, and DRC
is at 12 violations, all of them silkscreen cosmetics.

*(These counts include the post-verification fixes and the decoupling addition below.
Before them the board had 724 segments / 168 vias / 175 footprints. The
"619 / 170" figure in earlier versions of this file was from before the chamfer
pass, which splits segments at every mitre.)*

The same thing happened again and again: **when a cluster resists
routing, the parts are facing the wrong way.** Swapping the two IF1 transistors,
swapping the limiter diodes, rotating the LO bias resistor 90°, swapping both
audio divider pairs, and moving the ADC divider to the socket each solved a
knot that no amount of trace shaping could. Six clusters had the same cause.

### Bottom-layer exceptions

Decision 2 (unbroken bottom plane) holds everywhere except the vb2 bias rail
under the IF strip (see the caveat below). 27 segments across 11 nets take a
short B.Cu hop, 136.5 mm in total (state after step F; it was 35 segments /
14 nets before the September 2026 cleanup). I list them here so a reviewer does
not have to search for them:

| Net | Length | Why |
|---|---|---|
| `vcc` | 44.0 mm (8 segments) | Supply pockets fully enclosed by signal routing (LO pair, oscillator collector, ×5 damping resistor). Two of the hops run 10–16 mm; step D2 removed the mixer-tank hop and step E2 shortened the oscillator feed. |
| `vb2` | 27.9 mm | The collector channels form north–south walls every 12 mm along the IF strip, and a DC bias rail cannot cross them on the front. **See the caveat below: these four are the ones that are not clear of the strip.** Measured in step F: hop 1 (131.75,74.65)–(141.6,73.05) 10.5 mm under b1; hops 2–4 (148.9→153.9 / 159.7→165.9 / 171.7→177.9, y ≈ 73) 5–6.2 mm each under b2/b3/b4 and the c1/c2/c3 risers. Every crossing pairs a riser with the base that the riser's own tank already drives through its tap, so it is feed-forward, not a loop. No slot is crossed by both an early and a late stage node. The return-current detour is 1.3–4.3 mm per crossing (< 3 nH, < 0.03 Ω at 12.9 MHz). |
| `n_rf2` | 23.3 mm | Its original west detour was a wall that would have forced **five** LNA nets to hop. One hop for this net is better than five for the others. |
| `blo`, `vb2n` | 6.1 / 6.9 mm | Q2's base and emitter share one SOT-23 face, but their nets arrive from opposite sides, and the e23 corridor blocks the region. Eight single-layer topologies were checked on coordinates, and each one only moved the crossing. |
| `a_e4b` | 7.5 mm | Local: every gap in the audio component row already had a net through it. |
| `audio`, `ln_c`, `ef3`, `y4` | 2.5–4.3 mm each | Same reason, one hop each. |
| `a_ce` | 6.4 mm | Two hops (2.9 + 3.5 mm) under the ESP32 socket, outside the IF strip. Step E3 removed the `vin5`, `adcref` and `adc` hops when it rebuilt the power entry and the ADC divider. |

All are DC or audio except `n_rf2`. **One correction to what this section used to
say:** the four `vb2` hops are *not* clear of the 96 dB IF strip. They run
at y ≈ 73, inside the SOT-23 row, and each one passes under a stage's input and output
traces, so they cut the plane exactly at the stage interfaces. That is a real weakness
against frozen decision 2, and I write it down here instead of hiding it. The
decoupling half of the problem was fixed by the per-stage 100 nF of step C and
the `vcc_if` split of step E3. The four hops themselves stay, measured and
justified in the table above (routing vb2 on the front would need the whole
SOT-23 row moved, which is a rev 2 job).

## Finishing

- **Corners** mitred to 45° where two segments meet at a right angle and nothing is
  anchored at the vertex. At 137.9 MHz the wavelength in FR4 microstrip is ~1.2 m,
  so a 0.5 mm corner is λ/2400. This is convention, it does nothing electrically.
- **Silkscreen**: the toroid outline crossed its own link pads, and its pin labels
  were 0.7 mm against a 0.8 mm minimum. The air-coil outline circle sat on its pad
  ring. Both are fixed in the library files and in every placed copy. Reference
  designators flagged by DRC were moved by a solver that scores candidate spots
  against pads, tracks, vias, other labels *and* footprint silkscreen. This took
  the silkscreen violations from 71 down to 12.
- **DRC rules** (`qfh_receiver.kicad_dru`): edge-launch SMA pads and their launch
  tracks are allowed to reach the board edge, but only inside two small named rule
  areas, so the check still works everywhere else. Library-sync mismatch is set to
  ignore, because it is a housekeeping check, not a manufacturing one.

### The test SMA "self-short" — a real bug, not a waiver

For most of the layout's history DRC reported that the test SMA's centre pad was
shorted to its own shell pads, and this was dismissed as an edge-launch artifact.
That was wrong. When the actual pad rectangles were computed, the pads turned out
to be **90° off**: the placement script had rotated the footprint without updating
the pads' stored angles (which in KiCad are absolute). The antenna SMA has the same
hidden error, but it looks correct, because at its 180° placement the wrong angle
and the right one give the same rectangle. The test SMA at 90° was really broken:
the pads overlapped each other, lay along the edge instead of across it, and could
not be soldered as drawn. The fix was to set the five pad angles to 180°. Each
shell also has a via now, tying its front and back faces to the plane (before,
they were "connected" only through the short).

I took two things from this. First, a violation that survives every pass is not
automatically a waiver: compute the geometry before classifying it. Second, a
script that rotates a footprint must rotate the pad angles too (the same bug was
caught and fixed for two other parts earlier, but the SMAs were placed before that
fix).

**DRC now: 11, unconnected 0.** The board is fully connected. The eleven remaining items are silkscreen cosmetics (labels in the mixer's densest column and in the cascode column, where three labels per 12 mm stage do not fit, plus D5's polarity marker).

## Sourcing pass — the catalogue check that found two bugs

I order the components from Farnell NL. Checking the board against a real
distributor catalogue, instead of against the values written in the schematic,
changed four things, and two of them were hidden design errors, not supply
problems.

**BFR93A is obsolete.** NXP does not make it anymore, and Farnell NL gives no
exact match for it at all. That is 15 parts: the LNA, the mixer, the oscillator
and the whole 96 dB IF strip. I replaced it with **BFR92P** (Infineon, order code by
manufacturer part `BFR92PE6327HTSA1`): same SOT-23 package, same B-E-C pin order
on pins 1-2-3, 5 GHz f<sub>T</sub>, and 15 V V<sub>CEO</sub> instead of 12 V. The
simulations never modelled a BFR93A in the first place. They used a generic
`NPN(BF=100, CJC=0.7p, TF=28p)`, which is an RF NPN of about 5.7 GHz, so nothing had
to be re-simulated. BFR92P is actually the better fit: BFR93A was optimised for
5–30 mA, BFR92P for 0.5–20 mA, and this board runs about 2 mA per stage.

**The specified trimmer ranges do not exist as products.** The schematic asked
for 1–10 pF and 5–30 pF parts. The real Murata TZC3 ranges are 3–10 pF and
6.5–30 pF. Checking every tuned circuit against the ranges you can actually buy
showed two capacitors whose fixed value was chosen as if a trimmer could
remove capacitance as well as add it:

| Tank | Needs | Was | Reachable? | Now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ×5 multiplier, 125 MHz | 10.8 pF total | `C3` = 10p | no, the floor is 11 pF | `C3` = **4.7p** |
| LO output, 125 MHz | 14 pF total | `C12` = 12p | only at the stop | `C12` = **8.2p** |

*(Replaced on 2026-09-25 by the values pass after layout step E, once the
board's own parasitics were measured: `C3` = **3.3p**, `C12` = **5.6p**, and
the BPF tap caps `C23`/`C27` = **8.2p**, `C24`/`C28` = **56p**. Order these,
not the values in the table above. The reasoning is in the Step E section.)*

The ×5 tank could not be reached even with the idealised 1–10 pF part. This
error stayed invisible as long as the BOM had a trimmer that nobody sells.

With the real parts and the corrected fixed values, every tuned circuit ends up
mid-range:

| Tank | Tuning range | Target | Trimmer sits at |
|---|---|---|---|
| Input BPF ×2 | 115.8 – 138.8 MHz | 137.9 | 3.20 pF, near the low stop |
| ×5 multiplier | 107.2 – 148.1 MHz | 125.0 | 6.11 pF |
| LO output | 113.5 – 136.6 MHz | 125.0 | 5.83 pF |
| IF ×7 | 11.6 – 13.6 MHz | 12.9 | 13.19 pF |

*(Trimmer positions above are for the pre-step-E values and an ideal netlist. On
the built board I expect ×5 ~5.4 pF, LO ~5.8 pF, BPF ~6.5 pF, each ≥ 1.9 pF from
either stop (see "Known constraints" and step E).)*

**Ordering data lives on the symbols now (step F).** Every schematic symbol
has hidden `MPN` / `Farnell` fields where a catalogue part was confirmed
(Murata TZC3P300A110R00 = Farnell 2456139 for the seven 6.5–30 pF trimmers,
TZC3R100A110R00 = 2456140RL for the four 3–10 pF ones, but confirm the cut-tape
code before ordering; Infineon `BFR92PE6327HTSA1` for the 15 BFR92P; Amphenol
132289 = 2112467 for the SMAs). It also has a `Spec` field for what a distributor
needs beyond the value: **every pF-range capacitor is NP0/C0G** (an X7R 56 pF would
drift the IF tanks by ~±260 kHz over a room's temperature swing), nF-range
parts are X7R, the electrolytics 10 V (D5/P2.5 for the 100 µF, D8/P3.5 for the
1000 µF), the crystal is a **fundamental-mode** HC-49/S (a 3rd-overtone 25 MHz part
would run at 8.3 MHz in this Colpitts), and each coil has its winding recipe. The BOM
is exported from those fields: `pcb/fabrication/qfh_receiver_bom.csv` (71 lines; the
four toroid variants stay on separate lines).

The BPF pair was the only position still close to a mechanical stop. Its
trimmer is the *only* capacitance across the tank. The divider (13.04 pF then,
7.15 pF since step E) is fixed by the impedance match and cannot be traded
against it. So the coarse adjustment there is the coil, exactly as SIM 7 says:
wind five turns and squeeze or spread them. If a coil comes out with too much
inductance, the trimmer cannot compensate, and I have to stretch the coil instead
of turning the trimmer. This block can be swept on its own on a miniVNA, so it is
also the block where this problem is easiest to catch.

**Trimmer footprints unified.** Seven positions had a Sprague-Goodman SGC3
footprint and four a Murata TZC3, but only Murata is stocked. The two footprints
are geometrically identical apart from the pads (±1.8375 mm / 1.075 × 1.4 mm
versus ±2.075 mm / 0.85 × 1 mm) and 0.12 mm of courtyard. All eleven are now
TZC3. No re-routing was needed: the pads move 0.24 mm outward, and the existing
track endpoints stay inside the new rectangles with 0.188 mm of margin. This was
checked geometrically on all 22 pads and confirmed by DRC: 12 violations and 0
unconnected, the same as before.

**Not available from Farnell:** the T37-6 iron powder cores (7 needed). Micrometals
amateur-radio core sizes are not in the catalogue, so I get them separately from
kitsandparts.eu or rf-microwave.com. Avoid UK suppliers because of post-Brexit
customs into NL.

This is the same kind of mistake as the SMA one above. A value in a schematic is
only a guess until something outside the design confirms it. An idealised trimmer
range hid a tank that could never have been tuned, and I would only have found it
on the bench, after paying for the board.

## Independent verification pass — two board-killing defects

Before releasing gerbers, I had the finished board verified again from scratch by eight
independent checks that ran against the files, not against the design intent:
schematic↔PCB net consistency, board netlist versus the golden LTspice netlist,
raw copper geometry (shorts and connectivity computed without DRC), footprint
fidelity against the library sources, zones and keepouts, component values and
polarity, RF layout quality, and a DRC/ERC/gerber export dry run. Every finding
was then given to a separate check that tried to disprove it by recomputing the
numbers behind it.

Thirty-three findings came back. Two of them meant the board could not work.

### The TO-92 transistors had base and emitter swapped

The schematic was generated by a script using KiCad's generic
`Q_NPN_BEC` / `Q_PNP_BEC` symbols for **every** transistor. For the fifteen
SOT-23 parts that is correct, because a BFR92P really is base-emitter-collector on
pins 1-2-3. For the five TO-92 parts it is not: a 2N3904/2N3906 is pin 1 = emitter,
pin 2 = base, pin 3 = collector, which is why KiCad's own `2N3904` symbol extends
`Q_NPN_EBC`. So the board sent the base net to the left hole and the
emitter net to the middle one.

This cannot be fixed at assembly time. **The base is the middle lead on
every TO-92 transistor made**, so no insertion angle and no substitute part can
save it. Rotating the device 180° only puts the collector on the base net. All
five audio stages (the Q5/Q7 differential pair, Q9, Q14 and the Q18 output)
would have been in reverse-active bias, and the ESP32 would have received silence.

Three independent sources agreed before anything was changed: KiCad's
own symbol inheritance, the datasheets of the real parts, and the board's own topology
(pad 2 of each part runs to an emitter resistor: R41 and R49 to ground for the
NPNs, R36 and R46 to `vaud` for the PNPs).

The fix swaps pads 1 and 2 on all five and reroutes both nets at each one. Four of
the five could be rerouted on the front layer with no via and no bottom-layer copper.
The emitter nets at Q9 and Q14 pass *through* the transistor pad as a junction, so
deleting the stub would have split the net in two, and those were rerouted around
the package instead. The schematic symbols were changed to the `EBC` variants,
which fixes the root cause: the two symbols have **identical pin
positions** and differ only in pin numbering, so not one wire moved.

There was one trap here. The five reroutes were designed separately and in
parallel, and two of them share a net: `a_b1` ties Q5's emitter to Q7's base.
Each design assumed the *other* transistor stayed broken, so when both were applied
they shorted `a_b1` to Q7's pad and left a dangling stub. Parallel design is safe
when the geometry does not overlap, and unsafe as soon as two workers touch the same
net. The combined route had to be drawn once, by hand, afterwards.

### The ESP32 socket could not have powered anything

J3/J4 are a 2×19 header at 25.4 mm, the DevKitC 38-pin footprint. Only five pads
were wired, and two of them were wrong. The board's whole 5 V feed was on the
module's **GPIO23**, and a ground landed on **EN**, which keeps the chip in
reset all the time. The module's real 5 V pin (row-A position 19) was not connected.

Before anything moved, the pinout was checked against the Espressif ESP-IDF DevKitC
V4 guide, a second pinout reference and the ESP32-WROOM-32 module pin list. All four
possible insertion orientations were also listed. Only one is physically possible
for a socketed module, and with it the three remaining anchors (3V3, GPIO36, GND)
are all correct. So the defect was certain and did not depend on orientation.
`vin5` now lands on row-A pin 19 and a second ground on row-A pin 14, with EN left
floating (the DevKitC has its own pull-up).

The root cause is still visible in the file: J3 and J4 were labelled "row A" and
"row B" the wrong way round, and that is almost certainly how the two wrong pins were
picked. Those labels are corrected too, otherwise the bug would come back on the
next edit.

Two notes for the firmware, not the board. GPIO36 is on ADC1, which is the unit
that keeps working while WiFi is on (ADC2 does not), so the pin choice is right.
And the 10k/6.2k divider sits at 1.26 V quiescent, which needs
`ADC_ATTEN_DB_6` or `DB_11`, because the default 0 dB range tops out at 950 mV.

### Also fixed

The default netclass track width was 0.2 mm, but the board minimum is 0.25 mm, so
any track drawn by hand in the GUI would have failed DRC as soon as it was drawn.
J2 now has a real `dnp` attribute instead of only saying so in its value field.

## IF strip decoupling — the constraint that only existed in prose

The "Known constraints carried from simulation" section above has always said:
*every IF stage gets local 100 nF decoupling on BOTH vcc and vb2*. The board did
not do it, and neither does `sim12`: it has exactly one `Cbyp_S` and one
`Cb2_S`. In a lumped netlist, eleven ideal 100 nF in parallel are electrically
the same as one. That is how a layout rule can stay unimplemented in a document
for months without any simulation catching it. As built, the four tank cold ends
were 34–49 mm from the nearest vcc-to-ground capacitor, and the four cascode bases
were 30–62 mm from the nearest vb2 one.

**Thirteen 100 nF 0805 capacitors closed it** *(as first placed in August 2026;
this changed twice since. C74/C77/C78/C75 moved to the front at (146.6 | 158.6 |
170.6 | 182.6, 71.25) in step C, and C66–C73 moved to y = 57 at x = tap − 5.24 /
+ 0.16 on the new nets `vcc_if1..4` behind the series parts R66–R69 in step E3.
C76 is unchanged)*:

| Part | Where | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| C66–C73 | front, y = 53.35, straddling each stage's vcc drop | two per stage, at x = tap ± 1.1 mm, in the empty 5.5 mm strip between the board edge and the vcc trunk |
| C76 | front, x = 115.99 | the corner where the vcc feed to the mixer tank leaves the trunk |
| C74, C77, C78, C75 | **back**, directly under Q8/Q13/Q17/Q20 | the shortest possible loop from each cascode base to the ground plane |

Two things about how this was decided. First, the initial proposal put vb2
capacitors at stages 1 and 4 only, saying that stages 2 and 3 were "not worth
rerouting". But when the two skipped sites were measured, they were geometrically
identical to the two that were accepted, so leaving them out was a choice and not
a real limit. All four stages get one.

Second, the proposal said the back-side ground pads would connect to the plane
"by thermal spokes, which is what makes this the lowest-inductance mounting
available anywhere on this board." **Both ground zones on this board are
`connect_pads no`**: the plane never connects to a pad, and that is exactly why
every ground pad here has its own stub and via. Built as proposed, those
four capacitors would have had a floating ground terminal and done nothing at all.
Each one now gets a short back-side stub to its own via, at positions found by
searching for clearance, not assumed (margins 0.35–0.60 mm).

A note for the bench: with a single 0805 and its via, the mounting loop is about
3.5 nH, so a 100 nF is already past self-resonance at 12.9 MHz and looks like
roughly 0.16 Ω. Two in parallel per stage is what gives the margin. It is not the
capacitance that helps (it does not matter this far above resonance) but the
halved inductance.

### The vb2 hop under the strip

The first of the four `vb2` bottom-layer hops ran at y ≈ 73.05–73.45, straight
through the transistor row, and crossed **b1** twice (b1 is the strip input, a
microvolt-level node). It now drops to y = 74.65, below the row.

This one was close, and the numbers decided it: the reroute halves the crossings
of b1 from two to one, but makes the plane slot longer, from 7.01 mm to 10.51 mm.
A slot forces a return-current detour of about its own length, so two crossings of
a 7 mm slot cost more detour (≈14 mm) than one crossing of a 10.5 mm slot
(≈10.5 mm). On top of that, the broadside overlap with b1 falls about four times.
Hops 2, 3 and 4 stay where they are. With a 100 nF now at each cascode base, the
vb2 rail between stages carries almost no signal current, so only the plane slot
is left, and moving those hops would make the slots longer without removing
crossings.

### Step C — the last parts leave the back side

My rule: no components on the back. The four vb2 decoupling capacitors
placed on B.Cu in September were the only ones there. There was no front-side spot
within 4 mm of the cascode bases without moving something, so the fix is the same
at every stage: **R15/R16/R19/R21 slide 1.25 mm north** (R16/R19/R21
also rotate 180° so the base pad faces west and the b_n trace leaves on a single
45° diagonal). The capacitor sits between resistor and cascode with its vb2 pad
2.4 mm from the base, and one GND via per stage serves both the resistor and the
capacitor. The vcc pass over each stage drops to y = 67.3, so the closest
rail-to-base-pad gap is 0.50 mm instead of 0.30.

The spec came from the audit. An independent adversary (an AI agent whose job is
to try to break a design) recomputed it against the live file. It found three
deletions the spec had forgotten (its new vcc horizontals would have been laid on
top of the old ones, which is exactly the "stitched over one another" look this
pass is meant to remove) and two deletions written in reverse orientation. It also
made the y = 67.3 variant the default, because it costs nothing. Before anything
was written to the file, each of the 48 deletions was checked to match exactly once.

Cost and benefit, measured: the base-to-plane loop goes 3.2 → 4.2 nH (remaining
base impedance 0.14 → 0.22 Ω at 12.9 MHz, ~1 % of the upper device's 1/g_m, so
negligible). In exchange, 8 vias and ~11.6 mm of bottom-layer slots leave the
plane under the 96 dB strip. Board: 753 segments, 183 vias, **0 footprints on
B.Cu**; DRC 12, all silkscreen, 0 unconnected.

Two more tooling traps showed up in this step, both of the same kind.
`R` and `Clear` are PowerShell aliases (`Invoke-History`, `Clear-Host`),
and an alias wins over a function with the same name. So a helper called `R`
never runs, and a placement solver whose test is called `Clear` rejects every
candidate without reporting anything. Also, `[regex]::Replace(s, p, r, 1)` has no
"count" overload: the `1` is `RegexOptions.IgnoreCase`, and it replaces
every match. Applied to a footprint block, it moved every pad onto the footprint
origin. DRC caught it, the file was restored from the pre-edit backup, and the fix
was a string edit that only touches the first match.
### Step D — the IF strip: measure first, then only what the numbers justify

**D1: tanks and risers.** The audit had asked for every tank capacitor,
trimmer and damping resistor to be pulled up to its toroid (they sat 22–31 mm
south, joined by long collector risers), because the risers ran 1.3–2.3 mm from
base nodes. The AI designer agent was told to measure again before designing, and
the numbers had changed. Step C had already put the base resistors' base pads on the
far side of the risers, so the same-stage riser→base coupling was 41–58 fF
(|T| 0.15–0.21), not the 81–119 fF / 0.43 the audit measured. In the remaining
1.85–2.25 mm parallel runs, each riser runs next to the *next* stage's base. Its
tank already drives that base through its tap, in phase, so this is feed-forward,
not a loop. Also, the full "tanks to toroids" pattern turned out to be impossible
on one layer for stages 1–3 (0.2 mm between consecutive toroid courtyards, and each
hot pad is boxed in on the north and east by its own tap line).

Only what the numbers justified was done. Stage 4 has a real free pocket east of
L6 and got the full pattern (c4 53 → 28 mm, tank loop 38 → 10.5 mm). Stage 1's
damping resistor R29 moved away from the injection leg (c1 copper now
≥ 8 mm from b1). The stage 2/3 risers were tucked into the resistor column, which
freed clean 2.55 mm corridors for the walls. Every collector stub now leaves
its SOT-23 along the pad axis, and the trimmer entries are centred. In total: 47
deletions, 32 additions, 4 vias, 4 moves, all reviewed by the adversary first (it
added a track endpoint at the stage-4 trimmer pad, where the spec's bus only
passed through it).

**D2: the walls, finally.** Frozen decision 3 promised via-stitching walls
with exposed copper strips for an optional brass sheet, and the audit found the
stitching was sparse exactly at the stage interfaces. Now they are built: an
east–west wall of 19 ground vias between the IF strip and the audio row (three
collinear runs at y 95.75 / 95.5 / 94.0, ≤ 5 mm pitch), a north–south wall of 10
between the mixer tank T1 and the IF filter T2, and stage-boundary columns of
9 / 8 / 9 vias at x ≈ 149.1 / 162.2 / 174.2. That is 60 new ground vias, each one
checked against **both** copper layers (a via through a bottom-layer signal hop is
a short) and against hole-to-hole spacing. Nine solder-mask openings expose the
ground pour along the walls. Each one was checked to lie on continuous ground and
to stay ≥ 0.5 mm from any signal copper, because a brass strip soldered over

The mixer|IF wall that the audit sketched at x = 117.5 could not be built (a 0.41 mm
corridor). Instead, the mixer-tank hot node was rerouted through the gap between

### Step E — front end, LO chain and supply: the last copper pass

This step covered three regions, each with its own independent AI designer agent.
Each design was then reviewed by an adversary agent that recomputed every number
from the file before anything was applied (all three verdicts: sound with
corrections, none blocking). The changes were applied by script in the order
E1 → E2 → E3, with a DRC after each one. Every deletion was checked to match
exactly one existing item, so nothing could be removed by accident.

**E1: RF front end (x 50–96).** The two BPF tank loops were the largest
current loops on the board. The trimmer and 15 pF cap were 22 mm from their coil,
and the return went through the plane hole under the air coils (the keepout
forbids pour on *both* layers, so a via return must go around a 26 × 16 mm
hole). Now the trimmer + 15 pF sit directly south of each coil. A 1.5 mm hot run
drops from the coil's hot pad to the trimmer, and a 1.5 mm ground strap runs
from the trimmer's ground pad back to the coil's cold pad, with one reference
via per tank just outside the keepout. The loop is now a 7 × 6.9 mm rectangle of
1.5 mm strips, ~12.6 nH total (≈ 8.6 nH on top of the coil itself), against 20–25
nH before. Hot-node copper is 9.9 / 9.7 mm (was 32.5 / 29.6), and the stray is
~0.5 pF (was ~1.6). The antenna launch is now one straight 5.45 mm coplanar line,
1.5 mm wide with the pour 0.3 mm on either side (50.8 Ω; 68.9 Ω where there is no
pour), and C23 is turned so its pad faces J1. Before, it was 17.7 mm in seven
segments. The BPF → LNA seam keeps the 50 Ω line and the R65 / J2 test topology.
The J2 launch narrows to 1.0 mm where it enters R65's 1.025 mm pad. The minimum
clearance of all new copper is 0.575 mm. After the refill, the front pour was
confirmed on both sides of the launch (point tests at x 51–55, y 62.6 / 65.4),
broken only by C24's stub at the C23 end.

**Values decided: C23/C27 15p → 8.2p and C24/C28
100p → 56p.** The layout showed a problem. With the real 3–10 pF trimmer,
82 nH coils and the loop's 8.6 nH + 0.5 pF, the trimmer that centres the filter
is 0.7 pF, and a +10 % coil cannot reach 137.9 MHz at any setting. The
designer agents offered two ways out: wind 4-turn coils (~55 nH), or drop the top
tap cap to 10p. Both were run, together with three more options, through the SIM 7
circuit with the layout parasitics, at critical coupling and with ±10 % coils
(`fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md`, SIM 7 rev 2, has the full table). The 4-turn coil
puts the trimmer at 6.8 pF, but it costs 0.7 dB of extra loss before the LNA
(loaded Q rises, and a 4-turn coil has less unloaded Q than the 150 assumed).
It also leaves only 1.3 pF to the top stop for a −10 % coil, and it changes the
winding recipe that the footprint, keepout and coupling spacing were designed
around. 10p over 100p changes the tap ratio to 0.091, and that has a price:
4.1 dB loss and the other Meteor frequency 3 dB down. Scaling *both* tap caps
keeps the ratio (8.2/64.2 = 0.128 against 15/115 = 0.130). So the loaded Q, the
5.5 MHz passband, the 2.2 dB loss and the 40 dB image rejection stay the same as
the rev 1 filter. The divider's series capacitance just falls from 13.0 to 7.15 pF
and gives the difference to the trimmer: **6.7 pF nominal (6.3 pF with the 1 pF of
stray that step F recomputed from the copper widths), 5.6–8.1 pF across ±10 % coils**.
That is at least 1.9 pF from either stop, with 1 pF still in hand if the stray
copper is 1 pF instead of 0.5 (the 10p/68p split, the other option that keeps the
ratio, would sit on the 3 pF floor in that case). sim12 and `sim7_input_bpf.asc`
have 8.2p/56p. Their ideal-netlist trimmer reads ~8.7 pF because the parasitics
are not in the netlist. A second-order note for alignment: the 56 pF tap caps'
ground returns through the plane around the hole (10.6–12.3 mm, ~1.5–2 nH in
series with −j20.6 Ω), so the effective tap-arm capacitance is ~60–61 pF and
the tap ratio is ~0.12 instead of 0.128.

**E2: LO chain (x 84–120).** Both LO tanks became mirror-image "two-rail"
stacks hanging straight off their coil pads, with every part just outside the coil
courtyard. The ×5 trimmer C4 is 7.3 mm from each coil pad (was 12.9 / 13.8),
C3 and R59 bridge the same two rails, and Q4 is turned so its collector points
2.2 mm up the hot rail. xtank1 copper went 35.7 → 15.1 mm, stray 1.96 → 0.85 pF,
and the closed tank loop 24.7 → 17.0 nH. There are two new parts. **C81** (100 nF,
`Cbyp_X`) sits on the ×5 tank's vcc node, so the class-C collector current now
closes in a ~6 × 9 mm loop instead of running 20 mm along vcc (12.4 nH, 9.8 Ω at
125 MHz) into the LNA's decoupling. **C82** (100 nF, `Cbyp_O`) sits where the
oscillator/buffer supply enters. The adversary caught a ground stub drawn 0.2 mm
short of C81's pad (the designer agent used the wrong 0805 pitch). That would have
been one unconnected pad on a supply bypass, and it was fixed before applying.
Minimum clearance is 0.53 mm; bufc grew from 11 to 25 mm (base network, accepted);
vcc's bottom-layer share fell 46.2 → 44.0 mm.

**Values decided: C3 4.7p → 3.3p, C12 8.2p → 5.6p.** The tank totals
that SIM 5/6 tune to (10.8 pF on the ×5 tank, 14 pF over 47 pF at the LO tap)
are what the trimmer is *turned to*, so the simulations do not change. Only
the split between the fixed cap and the trimmer changes, and that split has to
leave the trimmer inside its 3–10 pF window for any coil I actually wind. The
layout adds ~11 nH of coil legs (161 nH effective instead of 150), Q4's ~0.5 pF
Ccb and the 0.85 pF of stray copper measured in E2. Counting all of that, the ×5
tank needs 8.7 pF of external capacitance at 125 MHz. With C3 = 4.7p the trimmer
would sit at 4.0 pF (3.0 pF for a +10 % coil, on the stop). With 3.3p it sits at
5.4 pF, 4.6–6.5 pF across ±10 % coils. At the LO tap the same calculation gives
9.2 pF total through the 47 pF bottom cap, so 11.4 pF for the top arm. With
C12 = 8.2p the trimmer would need 3.2 pF (~2 pF for a +10 % coil, out of reach);
with 5.6p it needs 5.8 pF, 4.6–7.5 pF across ±10 % coils. One consequence for
alignment: the parasitics now supply part of the tank capacitance, so the top arm
at resonance is ~11.4 pF instead of the 14 pF the sim assumes. This makes the LO
tap ratio ~0.195 instead of 0.23, and the mixer sees ~1.4 dB less LO drive than
SIM 8 modelled. That is still inside the 0.1–0.8 V window SIM 8 stepped through.

Still left for later: an image-noise trap between LNA and mixer (SIM 7's 39.5 dB is
enough for rev 1).

### Step F — the finished board, verified by strangers, and what it changed

I had the finished files checked by six independent AI verification agents, each
looking through one lens (connectivity and netlist truth; clearance
and manufacturability; RF, impedance and noise; document consistency; ESP32
and mechanical fit; assembly, silkscreen and fab outputs). Each one read the
files with its own parsers. Every finding rated major or above was then given to
two more agents, which were told to disprove it or to judge its consequence.
Result: 41 findings, none critical, for 3.3 M tokens. The checks that passed are as
useful as the defects, so I summarise them first.

**Confirmed clean (numbers recomputed from the files, not from this README):**
one B.Cu plane island (11 839 mm², 87.7 % of the board), zone fills current
(the `.kicad_pro`/`.kicad_dru` must travel with the board, because refilling without
them moves the plane edge by 0.2 mm); 117 nets each one copper component, 0
dangling ends, 0 shorts; every GND pad on its own stub and via (median 1.9 mm);
schematic ↔ board 418 nodes / 0 mismatches, the 46 board-only pads being the
socket's unused pins and the toroids' empty tap/link pins; `vcc_if1..4`
membership exact; 121 R/L/C values identical to sim12; no via in an air-coil
keepout; hole-to-hole ≥ 0.70 mm; copper ≥ 0.30 mm from the edge except inside
the two SMA rule areas; 0 courtyard overlaps; the nine F.Mask ground strips on
continuous ground only; the socket matches the DevKitC V4 38-pin pinout (3V3 /
EN / GPIO36 / GND / 5V on row A pins 1 / 2 / 3 / 14 / 19, GND on row B pin 1;
EN floats safely on the module's own pull-up); gerber, drill and BOM exports
run.

**What was wrong, and the fixes applied:**

- **The LNA emitter bypass was 20 mm from the transistor.** This was the one
  finding that changed the receiver. The "Known constraints" bullet asked for
  RE1/CE2 tight to Q1, but the August routing had C49 at (92,83.8) with 8.1 mm of
  emitter track to R37 and 10.3 mm more to the cap: ~12 nH in series with 3.5 Ω.
  SIM 9 with that inductance gives S21 10.6–11.6 dB instead of 15.75, and an input
  of 66–73 + j27–31 Ω (VSWR 1.7–1.9) instead of 35 − j13. The BPF would have been
  terminated wrongly, and the bench acceptance test of frozen decision 4 could not
  have been passed.
  Fixed: R37 rotated under the emitter at (102.06,78.0), C49 beside it at
  (100.05,77.96) with a new via at (100.05,75.35), R42 moved west to
  (97.3,78.91) with its own via at (96.39,77.3), and the old runs and stubs deleted.
  Emitter-to-plane copper is now 5.2 mm (~4.4 nH with via and ESL), so I expect
  S21 ≈ 14.1 dB and Zin ≈ 47 + j9 Ω. The numbers are in `fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md`,
  SIM 9 layout note.
- **Both SMAs sat with the pad centre on the board edge**, so half of every 5.08 mm
  land was outside the board, and the connector's 4.45 mm legs would have reached
  C24's copper. J1 moved to (52,64) and J2 to (87,52), so the lands end 0.54 mm past
  the edge (JLC's ±0.2 mm outline tolerance never leaves a land short). C24
  moved to (57,67.2) with its via at (57,69.6) and a 45° stub from C23's pad.
  The J1 shell vias sit just past the pad ends at x = 55.0 (first placed at
  54.3, inside the pads, and moved in the re-check below). The SMA footprints'
  silk outlines were removed (they were off the board or over the launch parts),
  and the shell pads now connect solid to both pours (`zone_connect 2`) instead of
  through two half-wrapped vias. Expect a JLC DFM query about copper on the
  board edge at (50, 59–69) and (83–91, 50). The answer is "edge-launch SMA, by design".
- **Clearance floor raised to 0.254 mm** (project rules and Default netclass;
  hole-to-hole 0.5 mm; text stroke 0.15 mm). The eight gaps between 0.20 and
  0.25 mm that the old 0.2 mm rule allowed were cleaned up: the three vb2 vias
  beside R18/R20/R22 moved to (153.9 | 165.9 | 177.9, 73.2) with their hops
  re-anchored, the two vb2n segments past C37/C33 were narrowed to 0.35 mm, the
  lnk_b link route was re-drawn with true 45° jogs at y = 63.6 (0.45 mm to L1's
  pads), and C37's ground via moved to (103,82.4). The board-wide minimum
  different-net gap is now 0.269 mm, and DRC is 0 at the new floor.
- **Same-net notches** (sub-0.1 mm re-entrant gaps at width steps and chamfer
  corners that a fab's DFM flags as slivers) removed at seven places: a single
  straight vcc segment into the via at (116.5,89.3); the blo corner at R56
  re-drawn at 45°; the vcc corner at C63 rerouted from the corner instead of the
  pad; the bb entry at R3 made a pad stub plus a true 45° diagonal; the ef2 and
  vcc chamfers lengthened to 1.1 mm. The three "notches" at pads bridged by a
  narrower piece (R65, C7/C20, C23) are artefacts of the checker, so I left them.
- **R66–R69 fitted as 10 Ω, not 0 Ω.** The four `vcc_if` nets are fed from one
  0.5 mm trunk (0.6 Ω per 12 mm at 12.9 MHz). The reverse path stage-4 tank →
  trunk → stage-1 tank then has a loop gain of about −53 dB when each stage's cap
  pair looks like ~0.025 Ω, but only −10 dB if it were 0.2 Ω (a dry joint, a
  missing cap). With 10 Ω the trunk path no longer matters (−124 dB), and it costs
  only ~10–20 mV per stage, which is inside the ratiometric bias tolerance.
- **Silkscreen.** All 922 footprint silk lines were widened 0.12 → 0.15 mm (JLC's
  legend minimum), and the toroid pin labels "1"/"3" got a 0.15 mm stroke. The
  label solver learned two rules a fab cannot check: a reference may not sit inside
  another part's courtyard, and labels keep 0.3 mm from each other. With that, 62
  labels were re-placed (the IF caps' labels had been printing inside the toroid
  frames as "1 C67 3"; the re-check below found the solver had over-corrected
  and fixed 42 more). References with no legal spot are hidden: **Q13, Q17,
  C77, R16, R19, C78, C43** (the fab layer has them, and the custom toroid and
  air-coil footprints now have a `${REFERENCE}` on F.Fab too). Test-point
  legends added: GND at TP3, AUDIO at TP2, INJ (12.9 MHz injection) at TP1.
- **Fab settings.** Solder-mask expansion 0.05 mm (was 0; smallest mask dam
  left 0.555 mm); drill marks off in the plot parameters (they had put 114
  0.35 mm flashes on the paste and mask layers); assembly drawing:
  `kicad-cli pcb export pdf --layers F.Fab,Dwgs.User,Edge.Cuts`.
- **BOM.** See the sourcing section: MPN/Farnell/Spec fields on the symbols,
  `pcb/fabrication/qfh_receiver_bom.csv` exported from them.
- **Documents.** Frozen decisions 1 and 4, the footprint text (all 0805; the
  3-turn link on L1 and L7 only), the IF-decoupling table, the exceptions
  table (with the measured vb2 crossing inventory), the sourcing tables, the
  step E numeric drifts (LO tap 2 pF, C4 7.3 mm, C79/C80 2.2 mm) and the SimRef
  whitelist were corrected where the files contradicted them.

**Measured, recorded, not changed:**

- The ×5/LO coil pair (7 turns at 10 mm centre spacing) calculates to **k ≈
  0.05**, not the 0.03 the tanks were simulated with, because the coils are longer
  and their ends closer. SIM 5 re-run with K stepped: LO output 9.39 / 9.57 / 9.09 V pp at k =
  0.03 / 0.05 / 0.06 (±0.3 dB), while the ×5 collector swing *drops* 8.7 → 6.9 V
  pp, which means more breakdown margin, not less. The 5-turn BPF pair stays at k ≈
  0.03–0.04. If the LO tank shows a double hump at alignment, I can tilt or spread
  L11 on its legs.
- The WROOM antenna sits ~13 mm above both ground planes (Espressif asks for
  15 mm of clear space) and directly over the a_ce audio line and the vaud
  rail. The ADC parts are 1–4 mm south of its rectangle. This comes with frozen
  decision 6. WiFi stays off while recording, and if I ever want WiFi on during
  operation, a second 8.5 mm socket stack lifts the antenna to ~22 mm.
- Rail sharing with the ESP32: the bead + 1000 µF entry attenuates a 300 mA
  WiFi burst's ~0.2 V sag by only ~6 dB at 1–10 kHz (≈100 mV on vcc, 0.4 %
  of the rail, ~−48 dB AM-equivalent at the detector, a further −36 dB through
  the audio rail's RC). Fallback if a recording with a busy CPU shows kHz ripple:
  a low-ESR 1000 µF at C62.
- The four vb2 hops (27.9 mm) are still the only bottom copper under the IF strip
  (measured as feed-forward only, see the exceptions table). The n_rf2 hop (23.3
  mm, LNA → mixer) passes under two ground stubs whose return current goes
  around the slot. That is acceptable: one hop instead of five.
- BPF hot-node stray recomputed from the copper widths is 0.9–1.0 pF, not
  0.5 pF, so the trimmer nominal is 6.3 pF instead of 6.7, still ≥ 2 pF from either stop.
- No mounting holes (my decision). The board rests on adhesive standoffs
  or an edge-supported tray, and the micro-USB cable at x ≈ 229 needs strain
  relief at the enclosure, otherwise the socket pins would carry the lever load.
- The trimmer-position tables of the sourcing pass and the 37.6 dB of SIM 7
  rev 1 are kept as history. The current expectations are in "Known
  constraints".

**The re-check (26 September).** The AI agents that were supposed to review the
fixes above stopped on a spend limit, so at first the fixes were checked only by
the fix author's own scripts. Then one more independent AI agent recomputed every
changed area with its own parser and kicad-cli on copies. Its verdict: *ready with
minor fixes*. It confirmed with its own numbers: every net is one component; the
LNA emitter copper is 5.21 mm, with S21 **14.12 dB** at 4.4 nH in its own SIM 9 run
(15.75 at 0 nH, 10.71 at the old 12 nH); SMA lands 0.54 mm past the edge, legs
≥ 1.3 mm from foreign copper, shells joined to both refilled pours; board-wide
minimum different-net gap 0.269 mm; mask webs ≥ 0.555 mm; board, schematic and
sim12 values agree on every SimRef. What it found, and the fixes:

- **The BOM file was out of date.** It was exported before R66–R69 went to 10 Ω,
  so it would have bought 0 Ω links. Re-exported (and R49 is now 270 Ω, see below).
- **Labels.** The courtyard-aware solver had pushed 19 references 2.8–7.5 mm
  away from their parts (with another part nearer), six were still inside a coil or
  toroid outline (C7, C20, C4, C13, R15, C19), and three were off the board (R28,
  C31, J4). 35 were re-placed at the checker's positions. The four that its own
  positions made collide (R33/C19, C44 and R8 over a wall mask strip), plus three
  more found by a final audit (C18, C24, Q14), were placed by a nearest-legal-spot
  search. An audit of all 189 visible labels found none centred inside another
  part's outline, none with another part clearly nearer, and none off the board.
- **Solder wicking at J1:** the shell vias at x = 54.3 had their drills inside
  the exposed shell pads, under the leg tips. Moved to (55.0, 59.75 / 68.25),
  0.26 mm clear of the pad. J2 (DNP) has the same geometry, so move its vias to
  y = 55.0 if it is ever fitted.
- **Two re-drawn corners were not 45°** (37.7° and 43.6° vcc diagonals at
  (116.5,89.3) and (220.05,89.5)): re-drawn as true 45° pieces.

**R49 (RE4A) 150 → 270 Ω.** The TODO's "2× audio overdrive, raise RE4A to
330 Ω" turned out to be out of date, because SIM 2 rev 3b had already fixed that
in August. But at the real 4.6 V rail, Q4 came within 0.43 V of saturation at the
high end of the detector's level range. 270 Ω doubles the headroom (≥ 0.92 V worst
case) and keeps ≥ 380 ADC counts at the lowest drive. The full table is in
`fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md` under SIM 13. SIM 13 re-run with 270 Ω: THD 1.1 / 1.8 %, 0.45 Vpp at the ADC.

**State after step F and its re-check: 683 segments, 235 vias, 196 footprints
(all front), bottom-layer exceptions 27 segments / 136.5 mm on 11 nets, DRC 0
at a 0.254 mm clearance floor, 0 unconnected, schematic ↔ board 418 nodes /
0 mismatches, every pad angle matching its library footprint.** Next: gerbers.
