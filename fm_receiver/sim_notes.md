## sim1a_slope_scurve.asc

*Now: SIM 1a - slope detector S-curve (rev 5). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 1 - slope detector'.*

SIM 1a rev2 - slope detector, static S-curve (detected DC vs carrier frequency) REV3: rev1 found the tank detuned low by the detector; rev2's -2.2p (datasheet Cjo) was not enough - measured peak 12.84 MHz -> total parasitic ~3.3p (junction plus diffusion capacitance while the diode conducts). C1 = 65.4p puts the tank at ~12.94 MHz, the optimum detune for the MEASURED Q~122. On the bench this is the trimmer alignment. Two stepped measurements now: Vtank (true tank resonance + drive level, separates tank problems from detector problems) and Vdet (detected DC = the S-curve). Run, then View > SPICE Error Log > right click > Plot .step'ed .meas data. Pass: Vtank peak clearly resolved; Vdet flank steep and quasi-linear through 12.90. REV4: maxstep 5n -> 1n + compression off + .save, after the timestep study (sim1c/1d) showed the 5n run inflated the response ~2x - the diode's conduction pulses and its nonlinear junction capacitance need ~80 points per carrier cycle to integrate honestly. REV5: the CONVERGED S-curve put the resonance at 13.08 MHz with a 520 mV peak - the diode really adds only ~1.9p (not the ~3.3p the coarse 5n run faked) and loads the tank lightly. C1 trimmed 65.4p -> 66.7p, target f0 = 12.955 MHz (+55 kHz above the carrier, the max-slope point for the measured Q ~80). Sweep narrowed to 5 kHz steps. Lesson kept for the README: never trim a design against an unconverged simulation.

## sim2a_audio_freq.asc

*Now: SIM 2a - audio low-pass filter (rev 3b). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 2 - audio filter and ADC scaling'.*

SIM 2a - audio low-pass filter and ADC scaling: FREQUENCY RESPONSE (.ac).

WHAT THIS STAGE IS FOR. The slope detector delivers ~60 mVpp of 2400 Hz on a 0.23 V pedestal, behind ~150k. That is the APT subcarrier, and its amplitude IS the picture: the ESP32 samples this audio at 44.1 kHz and software turns the envelope into pixels. So this block has exactly two jobs - anti-alias filtering before that sampler, and enough gain to use the ADC's range.

WHY ACTIVE AND NOT JUST RC. APT occupies 320-4480 Hz (2400 Hz subcarrier +/- the 2080 Hz pixel rate), and Nyquist for 44.1 kHz sampling is 22 kHz. Three cascaded passive RC poles at fc = 6 kHz only reach ~20 dB at 22 kHz - soft skirts, no resonant poles. Two Sallen-Key sections give a 4th-order Butterworth: ~-46 dB at 22 kHz with under 1 dB of passband ripple, using two transistors. Section 1 (Q2, NPN follower): 10k/10k with 2.7n/2.2n -> Q=0.55, fc 6.5 kHz. Section 2 (Q3, PNP follower): 7.5k/7.5k with 8.2n/1.2n -> Q=1.31, fc 6.8 kHz. Together: Butterworth, corner ~6.6 kHz.

WHY A DARLINGTON AT THE INPUT, AND WHY PNP. The detector's 150k source impedance is brutal: a single transistor's base current through it would shift the detector's own operating point by ~0.3 V - moving the carrier off the slope and wrecking SIM 1's linearity. A Darlington cuts that to ~2 mV. PNP because the chain is DC-coupled and a PNP follower shifts the level UP by one Vbe while an NPN shifts it DOWN. Alternating PNP-NPN-PNP keeps every stage biased inside a single 5 V rail with no coupling capacitors and no negative supply. Starting at 0.23 V: 1.36 V -> 0.74 V -> 1.41 V.

(Not 0.7 V per junction: Vbe = VT*ln(Ic/IS), and Q1a carries only Q1b's BASE

current - about 2 uA - so its Vbe is ~0.50 V while Q1b's, at 0.46 mA, is ~0.64 V. The Darlington shift is +1.13 V, not +1.30 V.)

NOTE ON THE DRAWING: LTspice's PNP symbol sits collector-up, so on Q1a, Q1b and Q3 the GROUND flag is above the device and the supply below it. That looks upside down next to the NPN stages but is electrically right - a PNP's emitter belongs at the positive end. Supply and ground are net labels throughout rather than long rails, which keeps the signal path readable.

GAIN STAGE Q4: common emitter, 150R unbypassed sets the gain by feedback. (REV 3b) Ic ~ 0.85 mA, re ~ 30 ohm, collector load 3.3k in parallel with the 10k/6.2k divider = 1.9k, so Av ~ 9.7. 120 mVpp in becomes ~1.17 Vpp at the ADC - about a third of the 3.3 V range, all inside the ADC linear window. Without this stage the ADC would see 60 mV out of 3.3 V: barely 6 effective bits. CC1/RD1/RD2 re-centre the signal on 1.26 V (the linear region of the ESP32's own 3.3 V rail) and CADC is the last anti-alias pole right at the pin.

CHANGED FROM THE .cir VERSION: supply 5 V -> 6 V, so the whole receiver runs off one rail, and RE4B 3.3k -> 2.7k. The second change came out of a review that actually ran the circuit: with 3.3k the collector sat at 3.69 V, high on a 6 V rail and lopsided for the swing. 2.7k lands it at 3.23 V and drops the in-band THD from 4.66 % to 3.73 % for free - the filter response is unchanged.

PASS (rev 3b): |V(adc)| flat within 1 dB over 400-4400 Hz; >= 30 dB down at 22 kHz (expect ~46-47); the 12.9 MHz IF ripple region annihilated (>100 dB). Gmid is the mid-band gain - it should read ~10-11 (~20 dB) into the ADC node.

REV 2 - BACK TO 5 V (see the README's supply-rail section: the .cir was 5 V, the .asc conversion inherited 6 V, and now the whole receiver runs 5 V). The bias ladder is anchored to the detector pedestal through junction drops, so it does not see the rail; only the PNP follower currents (0.46 -> 0.36 mA, harmless) and Q4's collector DC change. RE4B stays 2.7k rather than reverting to the .cir's 3.3k: THD is dominated by re modulation, which falls with bias current - 2.7k keeps Ic at 0.27 mA, and the swing still has 1.3 V of down-room against 0.76 V of need. Filter shape: rail-independent, unchanged.

REV 3 - THE ESP32 MODULE INTERFACE, and a re-characterization. Decision: the receiver feeds an ESP32 dev MODULE (USB, easy to program and procure), not a bare chip. Three consequences, all handled here. (1) ADC SOURCE IMPEDANCE. The old 47k/47k divider presented 23.5k. The ESP32 SAR ADC wants <10k: with 23.5k and CADC 470p the pin charges with tau = 11 us against a 22.7 us sample period - two time constants, i.e. a sample-dependent error landing straight on picture brightness. (2) ADC LINEARITY. On 1.65 V the old 1.5 Vpp swung to 2.4 V, hard against the ESP32 ADC nonlinear region above ~2.45 V. New divider 10k / 6.2k puts the pedestal at 1.26 V and the swing at 0.6-1.9 V - inside the linear part. Source impedance drops to 3.83k, and supply rejection at the ADC improves from 70 % to 28 % because the collector now works into a lower load. CADC 470p -> 2.2n: a bigger charge reservoir for the SAR, and a 26 kHz pole. (3) SUPPLY NOISE. The module 5 V pin is USB VBUS - laptop SMPS noise plus the ESP32 own 250-500 mA WiFi bursts through cable resistance, i.e. tens to hundreds of mV, with content in the audio band. This block has poor PSRR by construction (ripple reaches the collector through RC4), so it now gets its own rail: RSUP 100 + CSUP 100u = a 16 Hz corner, -43 dB at 2400 Hz, for 0.11 V of drop at the 1.1 mA this chain draws. On the PCB put 100n in parallel with CSUP (an electrolytic alone is useless above ~100 kHz), and keep WiFi off during a pass - record first, transmit afterwards. ALSO REV 3: the drive is now the level SIM 13 actually MEASURED at the detector with the real limiter driving it - 120 mVpp on a 0.31 V pedestal, not the 60 mVpp / 0.23 V that SIM 1 predicted from an idealized source. The real limiter drives the detector tank harder and the S-curve slope grows with drive. Gain therefore falls from ~28 to ~11, which is the point: the 2x overdrive found in SIM 13 is absorbed by the same divider change.

REV 3b - Q4 OPERATING POINT, after the rev 3 run measured 7.5 % THD. Root cause, from the numbers: with 120 mVpp in and re = 86 ohm against 150 ohm of degeneration, v_be swung +/-22 mV against a 25.8 mV V_T - the emitter current was modulated +/-85 %, so re itself swung 47..577 ohm. H2 7.46 % vs H3 0.78 % confirms re modulation, not clipping. WHAT IT ACTUALLY COSTS: the picture lives in the ENVELOPE of the 2400 Hz subcarrier, and H2 at 4800 Hz is outside the envelope band. The metric that matters is gain compression: 10.17 small-signal vs 9.83 large-signal = 3.3 % at full white, which the APT calibration wedge corrects anyway. So rev 3 was usable - but the operating point had become tight (V_CE fell to 0.46 V on negative peaks), so two resistors buy margin AND linearity: RC4 10k -> 3.3k and RE4B 2.7k -> 820. Q4 current rises 0.30 -> 0.81 mA, re falls 86 -> 32 ohm, v_be swing halves to +/-11 mV (0.43 of V_T), the collector sits at 2.2 V and V_CE bottoms at 0.83 V instead of 0.46. Gain is deliberately unchanged (~9.7, ~1.17 Vpp out) - this buys linearity and headroom, not level. Cost: 0.5 mA and 0.06 V more drop across RSUP.

## sim3_if_filter.asc

*Now: SIM 3 - IF filter at 12.9 MHz. Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 3 - the IF filter'.*

SIM 3 - IF filter, 12.9 MHz double-tuned with MAGNETIC coupling. The two tanks share NO galvanic path - the only link is the K statement. That is deliberate: SIM 5 proved capacitive top-coupling has a trap (the coupling cap resonates in series with the below-resonance, inductive-looking second tank and peaks the transfer exactly where rejection was wanted).

DESIGN MATH. Target: 12.9 MHz centre, ~200 kHz -3 dB bandwidth. That is far wider than the 43 kHz the APT signal occupies (Carson: 2*(17k dev + 4.4k audio)), on purpose - the extra room absorbs the Doppler baked into the recording (+/-3.5 kHz), crystal tolerance and alignment error, and a slope detector needs the whole FM spectrum undistorted. L = 2.2 uH -> Z0 = w0*L = 178 ohm; C = 1/(w0^2 L) = 69.2 pF. Coil Q_unloaded ~ 178 (toroid) = Rser 1 ohm -> its own parallel loss is Q*Z0 = 31.7k. With 33k of source/load in parallel, each tank ends up at Q_loaded ~ 91, and BW(-3dB) = sqrt(2)*f0/Q = 201 kHz. Critical coupling is k = 1/sqrt(Q1*Q2) = 1/91 = 0.011.

THE .step IS THE POINT: k = 0.005 (undercoupled - narrow, low transfer), 0.011 (critical - flat top, maximum transfer), 0.022 (overcoupled - two humps, wider with steeper skirts). Plot all three on one graph: this is the coupled-resonator behaviour, measured instead of taken on faith, and it tells us how touchy the coupling adjustment will be on the bench.

REAL BOM (this is a PCB, so nothing exotic): L1,L2  = 27 turns of 0.4 mm enamelled wire on a T37-6 toroid (AL = 3.0 nH/N^2). Toroids are SELF-SHIELDING - important with 75 dB of IF gain on one board. Leads go straight through two PCB holes. C1,C2  = 56 pF NP0 fixed + 5-30 pF trimmer (target 69.2 pF, aligned). RS     = the mixer drain's own output resistance (~33k for a dual-gate MOSFET); no physical part, it is what loads tank 1. RL     = the first IF stage TAPPED DOWN, not a resistor: a 2N3904-class stage at 2 mA has r_pi ~ 1.3k, so the base connects to a tap at ~20% of the turns. SUPERSEDED at integration (SIM 11): the real cascode input is ~6.1k, so the BUILD tap is 13/27 = 48% (the same 0.507u+0.588u coil as every SIM 4 tank). Tapping down is what keeps a low-impedance transistor from destroying the Q. k      = a link winding of ~3 turns on each toroid, the two links wired together (still no galvanic path between the tanks). Turns are trimmed at alignment: one peak = critical, two peaks = too many.

PASS: centre within ~50 kHz of 12.9 MHz and symmetric; BW at k=0.011 in the 150-250 kHz band; >= 20 dB down at +/-500 kHz and >= 30 dB at +/-1 MHz; the three k values reproduce the three coupling regimes. Record V129 for the gain budget - the insertion loss here is paid back by the IF amplifier. If the BW measurement errors out (it references Vpk), read the -3 dB points off the plot with the cursors instead - the FIND measurements stand alone.

## sim4a_if_gain.asc

*Now: SIM 4a - IF amplifier chain (rev 3: four cascode stages, 5 V). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 4 - the IF amplifier and limiter'.*

SIM 4a rev 3 - IF amplifier chain: FOUR cascode stages on a 5 V rail.

TWO CHANGES FROM REV 2, made together on purpose.

(1) 6 V -> 5 V. Honest history: 6 V was never an architectural choice, it was a repair. In SIM 5 the multiplier's collector swung 17.5 Vpp, past a real BFR93A's 12 V Vceo (a tuned collector swings ABOVE the supply - flywheel effect), so the rail came down to 6 V and then got copied everywhere without being reconsidered. 5 V is the better rail: it is what USB delivers and what the ESP32 already runs on, so the whole receiver takes one cable and no extra regulator. Nothing in this block wanted 6 V.

(2) A FOURTH stage, +23 dB. With the mixer measured at 22.7 dB (not the 12 dB originally assumed), the end-to-end budget becomes -2.2 +15.9 +22.7 -6.2 +93 = ~123 dB at design time (SUPERSEDED: SIM 11 measured the seam in situ; final budget 130 dB / -106 dBm) - i.e. what a QFH actually collects from a decent Meteor pass. That closes the 23 dB gap rev 2 documented. A second LNA stage was considered and REJECTED: it would drop the threshold to -115 dBm, below the ~-113 dBm noise floor, so the limiter would be fully driven by noise alone. Gain past the noise floor buys nothing and costs stability margin.

WHY THE BIAS DIVIDERS CHANGED. This is the block that made 5 V risky: a cascode stacks two Vce plus the tank swing. Keeping rev 2's dividers at 5 V would have left the LOWER transistor only ~0.69 V of Vce - too close to saturation to trust. Two changes fix it, both toward standard values: cascode bias 22k/18k -> 8.2k/10k: unloaded 2.75 V, and a stiffer divider (4.5k Thevenin instead of 9.9k) so the four upper base currents only pull it down 0.19 V -> vb2 = 2.56 V, upper emitter 1.84 V. base bias 47k/13k -> 33k/12k: at 5 V the old divider gave only 0.53 mA; this restores Ic ~1.04 mA, so gm_eff = 4.9 mS, slightly BETTER than rev 2. Result: lower Vce = 1.84 - 0.53 = 1.31 V, MORE headroom than the 6 V version had (1.24 V), and upper Vce = 3.14 V with +/-2.4 V of tank swing available against the ~1.5 V the limiter needs.

PASS: peak within ~100 kHz of 12.9 MHz; total gain (Gout - Vb1) 88-98 dB; -3 dB bandwidth 120-250 kHz (four cascaded tanks narrow it vs three - expect ~200 kHz, still 3x what the signal needs); the four stage gains within a few dB of each other; and Vb1 BELOW 0 dB - the regeneration tell-tale that condemned rev 1 and that the cascode fixed.

STABILITY WARNING, louder than before: 93 dB at one frequency on one board is a lot. The cascode removes the in-device feedback path, but layout, shielding and supply decoupling now decide the outcome, and no simulation can tell you about those. Plan on a shielded compartment for the IF strip with filtered supply feed-through - the classic construction, and at this gain not optional.

## sim4b_if_limiting.asc

*Now: SIM 4b - IF chain + limiter (rev 3). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 4 - the IF amplifier and limiter'.*

SIM 4b rev 3 - four cascode stages on 5 V: DOES IT STILL LIMIT? (.tran)

Same circuit as SIM 4a rev 3. Limiting is a large-signal effect - an .ac run cannot show it. A limiter's whole job is to make the OUTPUT AMPLITUDE INDEPENDENT OF THE INPUT LEVEL, so the slope detector converts frequency only and fading or AM noise is thrown away instead of becoming picture content.

THE STEP LIST MOVED DOWN BY 26 dB. Rev 2 (3 stages, 70.5 dB) needed ~400 uV to reach the diodes; rev 3 (4 stages, 95.8 dB) needs only ~22 uV. That 26 dB IS the whole point of the fourth stage - it is what moves full limiter drive from -77 dBm to -102 dBm on paper (-106 dBm with the SIM 11 measured seam) at the antenna, i.e. from "bench playback only" to "what a QFH actually collects from a Meteor pass".

Expected: 1 uV -> ~0.05 V (linear), 5 uV -> ~0.27 V (linear), 30 uV -> ~1.2 V (limiting), 300 uV -> ~1.2 V (hard limiting, early stages saturating too). THE LAST TWO BEING NEARLY EQUAL IS THE PASS CRITERION: 20 dB of input change must produce about 1 dB of output change.

The run starts from the DC operating point (no uic), so the 100 nF bypasses and 1 nF couplers are pre-charged and only the RF settles - four tanks of Q ~ 34, tau = Q/(pi.f) ~ 0.84 us each. Two spaced measurement windows PROVE settling rather than assuming it (the SIM 5b rule). NOTE: .tran must start at Tstart = 0 - a non-zero Tstart makes every .meas silently FAIL in LTspice, which cost one full debugging round earlier in this project.

PASS: Voutpp at 30 uV and 300 uV within ~1 dB and >= 2 Vpp; Voutpp_mid == Voutpp; Vc1pp scaling perfectly linearly with the input (limiting belongs at the END of the chain, not the start); the four emitter DC voltages steady across the range - one that walks under overdrive is rectifying, not amplifying; and Vc1adc ~1.85 V, unmoved, which is the cascode holding its job - that node standing still is exactly why there is no Miller feedback.

## sim5_multiplier_x5.asc

*Now: SIM 5 - x5 frequency multiplier (rev 4, 5 V). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 5 - x5 frequency multiplier (the LO chain)'.*

SIM 5 rev3 - class-C x5 multiplier with MAGNETICALLY coupled double-tuned output. REV3: rev2's capacitive top-coupling barely helped H4 (-18.3 dB): the series coupling cap + the below-resonance (inductive) second tank form a spurious series resonance near ~117 MHz that peaks the transfer right where H4 needs rejection - a classic double-tuned-circuit trap. Fix: transformer coupling (K1 L1 L2 0.03, near-critical k = 1/sqrt(Q1*Q2)), no galvanic path - on the bench this is a link winding / two adjacent coils, exactly like a vintage IF transformer. ## sim7_input_bpf.asc

*Now: SIM 7 - input band-pass filter, 50 ohm in and out (rev 2, Sept 2026). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 7 - the input filter'.*

SIM 7 - input band-pass filter, 137.9 MHz, 50 ohm in and 50 ohm out.

WHAT IT IS FOR. Two jobs, and only one of them is obvious. The obvious one: keep strong out-of-band transmitters out of the LNA - FM broadcast sits at 88-108 MHz and airband at 118-137, both far stronger than a satellite. The one that decides the frequency plan: IMAGE REJECTION. With LO = 125 MHz and IF = 12.9 MHz, the mixer converts BOTH 125+12.9 = 137.9 (wanted) and 125-12.9 = 112.1 (the image) onto the same IF. Nothing downstream can tell them apart afterwards - whatever arrives at 112.1 MHz is inside the receiver forever. This filter is the ONLY defence, which is why 112.1 MHz was chosen to land in the quiet gap between FM broadcast (ends 108) and airband (starts 118) - see the frequency plan in the README.

DESIGN MATH. L = 82 nH -> Z0 = w0*L = 71 ohm at 137.9 MHz; the tank needs 16.24 pF total. That total is split on purpose: 7.15 pF comes from a CAPACITIVE DIVIDER (8.2 pF in series with 56 pF) and ~8.7 pF from a trimmer (~6.7 on the board). The divider does double duty - it is part of the tuning AND it is the impedance transformer: the 50 ohm port sits at the junction, where the tap ratio is 8.2/64.2 = 0.128, so 50 ohm appears across the tank as 50/0.128^2 = 3.05 k (rev 1 had 15p/100p, ratio 0.130, 2.94 k). (A tapped COIL would need a tap at 13% of five turns - i.e. 0.65 of a turn, which does not exist. Capacitors divide continuously, so at VHF the divider replaces the tap. Same idea as SIM 3 and SIM 4, different hardware.)

With coil Q_unloaded ~ 150 (air core, Rser = 0.47) contributing 10.6 k, each tank ends up at Rp = 2.94k || 10.6k = 2.30 k, so Q_loaded = 32 and the coupled-PAIR bandwidth ~6 MHz (sqrt2*f0/Q; measured 6.0). Enormous next to the 43 kHz the signal occupies - deliberately: this filter is not there to be selective, it is there to reject a band 26 MHz away. A narrow front end would only add loss where loss costs noise figure directly, and would need retuning to move anywhere else in the 137-138 MHz satellite band. Critical coupling for Q = 32 is k = 1/Q = 0.031.

MAGNETIC coupling again, no galvanic path between the tanks - the SIM 5 lesson. The .step through k = 0.015 / 0.031 / 0.062 shows under-, critical and over-coupling, exactly as in SIM 3. A prediction written here before the run - that overcoupling would buy image rejection for free through steeper skirts - turned out WRONG: measured rejection at 112.1 MHz was 42.0 dB undercoupled, 37.6 dB critical, 31.6 dB overcoupled. Far from centre the attenuation is set by LOADED Q, not by skirt shape; overcoupling widens the response, which is the same as lowering the effective Q, so the whole curve moves outward. The skirts really are steeper - they just start further out. CHOSEN: critical coupling. Undercoupling buys 4.4 dB more rejection but costs 1.9 dB more insertion loss, and loss ahead of the LNA goes straight into the noise figure. 37.6 dB is already past the 30 dB target.

REAL BOM: L1, L2 = ~5 turns of 0.8 mm enamelled copper wound on a 5 mm former, air core, then squeezed or spread to trim (that is how you tune a VHF coil). Mount them about one coil diameter apart and with axes aligned; that spacing is what sets k ~ 0.03, and it is adjustable by sliding them. Ct1, Ct2 = 3-10 pF trimmer (Murata TZC3; ~8.7 pF here, ~6.7 pF on the board where the layout adds ~8.6 nH + 0.5 pF). C1a/C2a = 8.2 pF NP0, C1b/C2b = 56 pF NP0 - same 0.128 tap ratio as the 15p/100p of rev 1 (Sept 2026, README SIM 7 rev 2).

THIS ONE YOU CAN MEASURE. It is 50 ohm in and 50 ohm out, which is exactly what a miniVNA presents. Build it, sweep it, and compare the trace with this simulation directly - the first block of the receiver that can be verified on the bench with instruments you already own, instead of taken on trust.

PASS: centre within ~2 MHz of 137.9; IMAGE at 112.1 MHz down >= 30 dB from centre; 137.1 MHz (the other Meteor) within ~1 dB of centre; FM broadcast (108) and airband (118) well down; insertion loss at centre small enough that it does not eat the noise figure - record V1379 for the budget.

## sim8_mixer.asc

*Now: SIM 8 - mixer (rev 2, 5 V). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 8 - the mixer'.*

SIM 8 - the mixer: a single-balanced SWITCHING mixer (half a Gilbert cell, i.e. the top half of the classic SA602), built from three of the same QRF transistors validated in six other simulations.

WHY NOT A BF998 MODEL. The plan called for a dual-gate MOSFET, but LTspice has no BF998 model, and writing one by hand would make the simulation confirm whatever number was typed into it. A switching mixer's conversion gain comes from STRUCTURE instead: chopping the signal current with the LO multiplies it by a square wave whose fundamental is (4/pi)*0.5, so conv gm = gm_eff/pi,  Av_conv = gm_eff*Rp/pi with gm_eff = 1/(re+100) = 8.7 mS and Rp = 6.8k: ~19x = ~25 dB. A formula checkable against the run, not a datasheet act of faith. On the PCB either build it as drawn (3 transistors) or drop in a BF998 - RF, LO and IF interfaces are the same.

HOW IT WORKS. Q1 is a degenerated common-emitter transconductor: RF voltage in, RF current out of its collector (100R unbypassed keeps it linear and predictable, same philosophy as every stage in this receiver). Q2/Q3 sit on top as a differential SWITCH: the LO on Q2's base (Q3's base is the same DC bias, AC-grounded through Cb2) steers Q1's current left-right 125 million times a second. The signal current arrives at the IF tank chopped by the LO - and chopping IS multiplication: 137.9 x 125 -> 12.9 (+262.9, killed by the tank). Q3's collector goes straight to VCC: that half of the current is thrown away (the -3 dB a single-balanced design accepts for simplicity).

THE TANK stands in for SIM 3's first filter resonator: 2.2u + 69.2p at 12.9 MHz, damped to Rp 6.8k (loaded Q ~30, settles in ~0.7 us). The switch collector looks like tens of k - consistent with the 33k source SIM 3 assumed for the real filter.

THE DOUBLE .step ANSWERS TWO QUESTIONS IN ONE RUN (8 combinations): (1) ALO 0.1..0.8 V maps conversion gain vs LO drive. Expect a rise, then a PLATEAU once switching is complete (~0.2-0.3 V) - the SIM 5b lesson: park the operating point on the flat part, not on a cliff. Our LO chain delivers 1.68 V nominal on 5 V (1.02 V at the -20% corner), tapped to ~0.39 V on the board. (2) FRF 137.9 vs 112.1 MHz is the IMAGE demonstration: both are 12.9 MHz away from the 125 MHz LO, so both convert onto the IF with nearly EQUAL gain. The mixer cannot tell them apart, no mixer can - which is the entire reason SIM 7's front filter exists and why its 37.6 dB is the receiver's whole image defence.

BIAS (5 V rail): Q1 base 1.33 V (33k/12k), Ie ~1.7 mA through 100+220 (220 bypassed by 10n - low X at 137.9 AND 12.9 MHz); pair bases at 2.5 V (15k/15k, bypassed), so e23 sits near 1.8 V and Q1 keeps ~1.2 V of Vce. RLO 10k isolates the LO injection from the bypassed bias node.

PASS: conversion gain (from .four: fundamental amplitude / 5 mV) reaching ~19x = 25 dB and FLAT for ALO >= 0.4 (plateau, not cliff); image gain at 112.1 within ~1 dB of 137.9 (proves the point); Vifpp_mid == Vifpp (settled); Ve23dc ~1.8 V and steady across ALO (bias not rectifying).

REV 2 - THE 5 V RAIL. The 6 V figure was never a design decision (see the README); the whole receiver now runs from 5 V. In a STACKED stage the rail change cannot just be typed into V2: both dividers are ratios of VCC, so at 5 V the old values would drop Q1's base to 1.08 V (Ie down ~35%, conversion gain down ~2 dB) and the pair bases to 2.1 V (Q1's Vce squeezed under 0.9 V, flirting with saturation on LO downswings). Fix: recompute the dividers to hold the OPERATING POINT, not the resistor values - RB 47k/13k -> 33k/12k (1.33 V, the same pair used by every IF stage in SIM 4) and RT 18k/13k -> 15k/15k (2.5 V exactly). Same currents, same gm, same Vce as before.

## sim9_lna.asc

*Now: SIM 9 - LNA (rev 2, 5 V). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 9 - the LNA'.*

SIM 9 - LNA, 137.9 MHz, an EMRFD-style FEEDBACK AMPLIFIER: 50 ohm in and out.

WHY THIS TOPOLOGY AND NOT ANOTHER TUNED STAGE. Two reasons. (1) The SIM 7 filter was designed AND will be aligned against 50 ohm terminations - so whatever follows it must actually present 50 ohm, or the filter's response (and its image rejection) warps. This amplifier's input impedance is set by RESISTOR feedback, not by transistor parasitics, so it holds near 50 ohm across the band and from part to part. (2) Stability by construction: SIM 4 showed what parasitic feedback does to tuned gain stages. Here the deliberate NEGATIVE feedback through Rf swamps the parasitic path - the classic homebrew building block from EMRFD (Hayward/Campbell/Larkin), used by the dozen in every design in that book.

HOW IT WORKS. Two feedbacks set everything: Rf = 470 (collector to base, through Cf so it carries only RF, the DC bias staying independent) and RE1 = 2.2 unbypassed in the emitter. Gain ~ sqrt-of-neither: with the 120 ohm collector load and 50 ohm output termination, Av ~ 33/5 ~ 6.6, and the shunt feedback makes Zin ~ Rf/(1+Av) ~ 63 ohm, trimmed toward 50 by the bias divider's ~347 ohm (910||560) across the input. Expected S21 ~ 16-17 dB into 50 ohm (a couple more into the mixer's high-Z gate).

BIAS (5 V rail): Ie ~ 21 mA (LNAs run hot - large-signal handling for the FM broadcast leftovers that make it past the filter). Divider 910/560 -> Vb 1.83 V loaded, Ve 1.03 V across RE1+RE2 = 49.2 ohm, collector at ~2.5 V (check the DC op point: hover the collector node - it should read ~2.4-2.6 V).

MEASUREMENTS. G1379 is the 137.9 MHz transfer; S21 = G1379 + 6.02 dB (the AC-1 source behind 50 ohm delivers 0.5 V to a matched load, so 0 dB gain reads as -6 dB here). Vb1379 is the INPUT MATCH gauge: exactly 0.5 means a perfect 50 ohm input; 0.42-0.58 keeps VSWR under ~1.5:1, good enough for the filter. Zin = 50*Vb/(1-Vb) if you want the number itself. G20/G300/G700 map the frequency response: a feedback amplifier should be broadband and MONOTONIC - Gpk noticeably above G1379 would mean peaking somewhere, i.e. a resonance that has no business existing in this circuit.

NOISE, honestly: a feedback amp is not the lowest-noise topology (~4-5 dB NF), and LTspice NF numbers would not be trusted anyway (see README). At 137 MHz the galactic background (>1000 K) exceeds the receiver's own noise, and the bench goal - decoding a REPLAYED recording through attenuators - does not depend on NF at all. Match, gain, stability: those it delivers.

REAL BOM: all E24 (470/120/2.2/47/910/560), Q = BFR93A, caps 1n + 100n NP0/X7R. 50 ohm in/out = the second block directly measurable with the miniVNA, sweep against this plot after building.

PASS: S21 15-18 dB at 137.9 (G1379 between 9 and 12 dB); Vb1379 0.42-0.58; response monotonic, Gpk within ~1 dB of the low-frequency plateau; smooth rolloff at 300/700 MHz with no resurgence.

REV 2 - THE 5 V RAIL (see the README's supply-rail section). One resistor: RB1 1.2k -> 910, restoring Vb to 1.90 V so Ie stays at 21 mA - the old divider at 5 V would have dropped it to ~15 mA, costing ~1 dB of S21 and, worse, shifting the 50 ohm input match the SIM 7 filter depends on. The price of 5 V is headroom: collector at 2.5 V, V_CE 1.47 V (was 2.5 V). That is >300x margin over the worst signal this stage ever sees (strong local FM broadcast after the filter's -40 dB: ~4 mVpp at the output), and 31 mW of dissipation - cool for a BFR93A. Compression drops but stays tens of dB above anything the front end can deliver.

## sim13_fm_endtoend.asc

*Now: SIM 13 - FM in, audio out (end to end). Explained in fm_receiver/DESIGN_LOG.md, section 'SIM 13 - the money shot: FM in, audio out'.*

SIM 13 - THE MONEY SHOT: a true APT-format FM signal in, audio out at the ADC. Source SFFM(0 A 12.9Meg 7.083 2400) = 12.9 MHz carrier, FM by 2400 Hz at +/-17 kHz deviation - exactly what NOAA APT puts on the IF after the mixer. Injected at the IF-chain input because FM survives frequency conversion untouched; the RF half (+20.6 dB) is already measured in SIM 11, and putting 137.9 MHz in a 2 ms run would need 0.1 ns steps = 4e9 points, impossible. Chain under test: 4 cascode IF stages -> limiter -> RS 33k -> slope detector -> full audio chain -> ADC node. 1 ns steps, 2 ms, two amplitude steps. THE POINT OF THE TWO STEPS: 100 uV and 1 mV are 20 dB apart, both far past the ~22 uV limiting threshold. If the receiver works, V(adc) reads the SAME 2400 Hz at the SAME amplitude in both - limiting has erased the AM, which is why FM survives fading and why this architecture exists at all. THE ACQUISITION TRANSIENT (found by rev 1, kept as a documented finding). Starting from the no-carrier DC point, the pedestal steps 0 -> 0.31 V; the audio gain stage 100 uF emitter bypass holds the old bias, the whole step lands on the emitter degeneration and the stage saturates for the entire window (rev 1 read V(adc) = -0.55 V) while everything upstream was perfect. That is REAL receiver behaviour - the first ~50-100 ms of audio after a carrier appears are garbage while CC1/CE4 find the new pedestal, irrelevant in a 10-minute pass. Here .ic V(audio)=0.31 presets the settled state. EXPECTED (audio chain at rev 3b): Voutpp ~2.3-2.5 V both steps (clamped); Vauddc ~0.31, Vaudiopp ~110-130 mVpp; Vadcpp ~1.1-1.3 Vpp EQUAL between the two steps within ~1 dB - that equality is the whole point; Vadcavg ~1.26.
